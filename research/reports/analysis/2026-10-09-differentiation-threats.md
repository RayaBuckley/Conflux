## 7. Invalidation verdict per paper (grounded in named artefacts)

- **2610.04975 — PARTIAL, the only real novelty loss.** Invalidates novelty of version-bound witness refinement, atomic reserve/commit recheck and the memoryless-allowlist separation result; the `CLAIMS.md` row "Dynamic effects are re-authorised at execution time (Implemented)" becomes a replication. Survives: what it refuses by construction — one principal and one authorization root (A8; §2.5), and no derivation of provenance (§1.1, §9.5). Evidence: `docs/reference/SECURITY_MODEL.md` pointwise rule `Allow(a, PC)`, `docs/decisions/024-external-provenance-and-authority-bounds.md`, `src/conflux/domain/provenance.py`, `tests/test_provenance_precision_evidence.py`.
- **CaMeLoT — NO, adjacent layer.** Invalidates novelty of checking a generated plan before execution (already unsafe-listed); plan-time only, so runtime enforcement returns to CaMeL and the `SECURITY_MODEL.md` effect-time recheck stands. Scope must be stated honestly: `src/conflux/planning/verify.py` delegates to `src/conflux/verification/plan_ir.py` (IR-encoded invariants, `docs/decisions/023-ir-encoded-verification.md`), not model checking.
- **ToolFence — NO, different layer; kills argument-level novelty.** Per-argument authorization from a compiled blueprint with a deterministic monitor is published; Conflux's analogous check is in `tests/test_argument_visibility_attribution.py` and `tests/test_plan_ir_extensions.py`. Delta is label semantics: `pi(p_j) in {user, tool, model}` by traceability with an LLM judge granting capabilities, versus an ACS query over every influencing principal with model judgement outside the TCB (`docs/decisions/025-authority-confinement-semantic-judgement-delegation.md`).
- **arXiv:2610.06966 — NO, but the formal equal to beat.** Execution-boundary authorization soundness with stated assumptions is published. Survives: the authority source — arXiv:2610.06966 assumes contract fidelity for one trusted task and puts LLM semantic binding inside its assumptions, while Conflux derives the principal set checked by the ACS (ADR-025; `principal-context-monotonic` in `src/conflux/verification/plan_ir.py` - that invariant was deleted as vacuous on 2026-10-16, so the evidence here is the ACS authority rule in `docs/reference/SECURITY_MODEL.md`, not the plan IR).
- **ACE — NO, oracle-labelled provenance.** Trusted-information-only planning and static flow checking are published, but labels are human or registry input ("requires the user to label all information sources"; "registered application security clearances") — the class `03-planning-recent.md` §C defines. Conflux counterpart: `src/conflux/domain/provenance.py`, `tests/test_provenance_precision_evidence.py`.
- **POLARIS — NO, nothing security-relevant to invalidate.** It owns typed-plan governance (typed DAGs, validator-gated execution, guardrails before side effects), all already unsafe-listed, and never mentions an attacker. Conflux's planning evidence is utility-only and bounded (`CLAIMS.md`; `research/output/runs/planning-pilot-{1b,3b,7b}-v1/`, `research/output/runs/runpod-results/runpod-eval/planning/`).

**Aggregate.** One paper removes the mechanism novelty, three remove plan-time and per-call checking novelty, arXiv:2610.06966 makes the authority source the only defensible formal delta, and POLARIS shows typed planning is ordinary engineering. Surviving claim, one sentence: **authority is derived from the organisation's policy over every influencing principal rather than compiled from one trusted task, and the same check runs at effect time over a plan the agent may still rewrite.**

## 8. Unsafe claims (`PLAN-partC-programme.md` §F, one line each, re-checked against the six papers)

1. **Typed plan IR** — POLARIS ships typed plan synthesis with typed I/O contracts; ACE compiles the plan into an information-flow grammar. Say "the plan carries authority", never "we introduce a typed plan IR".
2. **Pre-execution plan verification against a policy** — CaMeLoT model-checks a generated plan; ACE analyses the compiled plan against a lattice; POLARIS type-checks candidate DAGs.
3. **Plan-following least privilege** — ToolFence enforces capability, per-parameter binding and budget per call from an authenticated-query blueprint; ACE binds app clearances at planning time.
4. **Monotone / attenuation-only authority** — 2610.04975 derives subgoal authority by witness refinement against a principal-approved root contract (§4.3, §5.2).
5. **Commit-time re-authorisation** — 2610.04975 reserves a normalised effect and commits it at one linearisation point with version freshness (Theorem 2, B1); arXiv:2610.06966 mediates every external effect pre-commit.
6. **Region-local plan repair** — CaMeLoT repairs from a counterexample; arXiv:2610.06966 repair and replanning preserve the contract; POLARIS has a bounded validator-gated repair loop (L_max = 3).
7. **Typed-block runtime guards** — ToolFence monitor plus typed blueprint; arXiv:2610.06966 WRAP admission; POLARIS risk-control gates.
8. **A residual-authority or progress metric** — the direct threat already has prefix safety `t in L_C` and `SafeFuture_C`; CaMeLoT has liveness-style properties.
9. **(new) First fine-grained / per-argument tool-call authorization** — ToolFence: 629 parameter-aware pairs, ASR 21.20% -> 0.20%, 3.80 pp utility drop.
10. **(new) First to prove authorization soundness at the execution boundary** — arXiv:2610.06966: Lemma (Authorization soundness) and Theorem (Task-level authorization soundness), App. B.2.2.

Items 9 and 10 are not in the §F list; the programme file should absorb them. Also unsafe to imply: that typed-plan governance is itself a security contribution (POLARIS reads the same design with no adversary anywhere).

## 9. What a marker will ask (five questions, honest answers)

1. **"2610.04975 requires principal approval of the structure enforcement uses (A8, §4.1). What plays that role for you, and can you still state prefix safety `t in L_C` for a computed label?"** The analogue is the organisational ACS plus the effect-time certificate, not a per-task approval: weaker in that no human reviews the plan, stronger in that authority exists independently of the task. Prefix safety over a whole execution cannot be claimed — the checked property is per-effect authority confinement against current policy at commit, with `src/conflux/verification/plan_ir.py` and `tests/test_policy_and_ites.py` as implementation, not proof.
2. **"Does the guarantee compose across principals and survive plan mutation after approval?"** The multi-principal rule is the design (`Allow(a, PC) iff for every p in PC, ACS permits p`; `docs/decisions/024-...md`), and 2610.04975 excludes exactly that case (A8; §2.5). Composition across independent authorization roots is not claimed and not implemented, and runtime delegation is disabled (`task-registry.json`: DIR-DELEG-001), so the answer stops there — do not borrow CaMeL's cross-agent non-composition result as support.
3. **"arXiv:2610.06966 assumes contract fidelity; you claim derived authority. Where is the derivation?"** In the policy kernel: the principal set accumulates along the execution path (`src/conflux/domain/provenance.py`) and an effect is admitted only if the policy permits every member. The honest limit is that ACS correctness and principal-set completeness are trust assumptions, stated in the `SECURITY_MODEL.md` TCB table and ADR-025, not proved.
4. **"ACE uses user labels, ToolFence traces `{user, tool, model}`. What is your label lattice, and who writes the policy?"** There is no separate lattice: provenance is a principal set and the policy is the organisation's existing ACS (Cedar policies; `tests/test_cedar_policy.py`, `tests/test_cedar_preflight.py`). That is the headline delta — the policy is not authored for the agent, and no LLM judgement sits in the authority path.
5. **"You claim the price of restriction is measured. What is your over-blocking number?"** Not yet comparable: Conflux reports bounded utility evidence with `security_violations=0` (`CLAIMS.md`; planning-pilot 7B 7/8, 3B 5/8, 1B 0/8; runpod 24-cell 19/24) but no false-block rate and no latency table against a baseline. ToolFence reports 3.80 pp utility drop and 1.63x-1.90x runtime; arXiv:2610.06966 attributes 813 utility errors and 218 false positives over 5,486 cases; CaMeLoT reports 16 of 75 disagreements. Until Conflux has that accounting, "measured price" is partial and the missing numbers must be named.
